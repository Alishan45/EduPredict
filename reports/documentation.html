1. Weekly Accomplishment Summary (10 Key Points)

During Week 02 of the Stack & Scale AI Engineering internship, the EduPredict machine learning system was conceived, implemented, evaluated, and documented following professional ML engineering standards. The following 10 points summarize the completed work:

  • 1. Dataset Ingestion & Archive Engineering: Acquired and verified the UCI Student Performance benchmark dataset across both cohorts—Mathematics (395 records) and Portuguese Language (649 records). Resolved a nested ZIP extraction bug in the data pipeline to guarantee seamless, zero-manual-intervention reproducibility.
  • 2. Pedagogical Target Formulation: Constructed a 3-tier discrete academic performance target based on Portugal's official secondary grading scale (0–20): 'Needs Support' (0–9), 'Satisfactory' (10–13), and 'Strong' (14–20). This avoids arbitrary thresholding while reflecting actionable educational intervention tiers.
  • 3. Data Hygiene & Quality Assurance: Conducted thorough data audits verifying 0 missing values, 0 duplicate rows, and correct type encodings across all 33 demographic, familial, and behavioral attributes.
  • 4. Strict Zero-Leakage Architecture: Enforced target leakage safeguards by completely dropping final grade G3 (the label source) and intermediate trimester exams G1 and G2 from pre-term feature matrices, preventing artificial grade interpolation.
  • 5. Leak-Free ColumnTransformer Pipeline: Encapsulated preprocessing inside a scikit-learn Pipeline. Handled numerical attributes with median imputation and standardization, and categorical attributes with most-frequent imputation and One-Hot Encoding, strictly fitted on training splits only.
  • 6. Majority Benchmark Establishment: Constructed a DummyClassifier benchmark predicting the majority class ('Satisfactory', 41.8% baseline) to ensure all machine learning models are evaluated against realistic statistical reference points.
  • 7. Multi-Model Architecture & Tuning: Engineered both a constrained DecisionTreeClassifier (depth=3, min_samples_leaf=5) and a regularized LogisticRegression classifier (C=0.1, L2 penalty), analyzing their bias-variance trade-offs.
  • 8. 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation: Conducted 5-fold stratified cross-validation on training data to establish out-of-fold generalization bounds (Decision Tree: 50.9% ± 3.9%, Logistic Regression: 47.4% ± 4.5%).
  • 9. Correlation Heatmap & Feature Selection: Generated Pearson correlation heatmaps that uncovered the strongest clean predictors (past failures r=-0.36, mother's education r=+0.22, higher education ambition r=+0.18). Pruning noisy attributes improved test accuracy to 48.1% (+6.3% over baseline).
  • 10. Multi-Tab Streamlit App & Dual-Timeline Deployment: Built an interactive Streamlit application featuring both Pre-Term Early Warning Mode (~48% Acc, survey only) and Mid-Term Tracking Mode (~89% Acc, G1/G2 included), complete with profile presets and responsible AI guidelines.

5. Error Diagnostics & Responsible AI Guardrails

5.1 Case Studies of Misclassified Students

Examining model misclassifications on held-out test data provides critical pedagogical insights into the limitations of tabular background surveys:

  • Case 1 — Student #111 (Actual: 'Satisfactory', Predicted: 'Needs Support'): The student had 1 past failure in an earlier grade, which triggered a penalty branch in the decision tree. However, the student compensated with high weekly study time (5–10 hours) and perfect attendance (0 absences), successfully achieving a satisfactory passing grade. The survey-level proxy penalized past failure without capturing compensating resilience.
  • Case 2 — Student #80 (Actual: 'Satisfactory', Predicted: 'Needs Support'): The student reported minimal study time (<2 hours), leading the model to suspect underperformance. Nonetheless, with 0 past failures and low absences, the student passed comfortably.

5.2 Responsible AI Principles & Ethical Limitations

Guiding Principle: EduPredict is an educational exploration prototype designed to help learners and educators explore pattern discovery. It must never be used for automated admissions, grading, tracking, ranking, or resource distribution.
  • Non-Causality: High absences correlate with lower grades, but punishing absences treats a symptom rather than addressing root causes (e.g., chronic illness or family obligations). Interventions must support the student, not penalize them.
  • Socioeconomic Proxies: Attributes such as parental education (Medu, Fedu) reflect structural societal inequalities rather than inherent intellectual capability. Using demographic attributes in high-stakes grading would reinforce historical disparities.
  • Context Specificity: The dataset originates from two Portuguese public secondary schools in 2005–2006. The learned associations cannot be assumed to generalize across diverse international educational systems or contemporary learning environments.
